🔎 Anchor Example 1
Plastic Bottles Anchor Example
without Golden Response (Prompt, Sources, & Rubric only)
Prompt
Your client recently started working on a business idea to reduce the number of plastic bottles that are not recycled. The idea is to commercialize a feedstock recycling solution for PET plastic, which is the most common plastic used in plastic bottles. Feedstock recycling is a way of breaking down PET back into its building blocks (monomers) using either chemical or biological methods. The big advantage is that new, high-quality PET can be created from these recovered building blocks. Your client would like an estimation of the world’s consumption of PET plastic bottles from January 2024 through December 2030. Assume that the average weight of an empty PET bottle is 10 grams.


Task Objectives:
1. Estimate the number of plastic bottles consumed from January 2024 through December 2030 using the following process:
   1. Using the files “828 - Important Plastic Water Bottle Stats.pdf” and “828 - Worldbank Population - Original.csv”, determine the average consumption of plastic water bottles per person per year based on global daily consumption and the world population in 2023. Round to the nearest whole number. Assume the figure has remained constant since 2023, and will remain constant through 2030.
   2. Using the file “828 - Worldbank Population - Original.csv”, determine the 2019 and 2023 World population and 2019-2023 compound annual growth rate (CAGR), rounded to two decimal places. Then forecast the World population in 2024-2030 using rounded CAGR, rounding the population to the nearest whole number.
   3. Using the calculations in 1a and 1b, determine the projected number of plastic bottles in 2024-2030, rounded to the nearest whole number.


2. Global annual demand for PET plastic bottles:
   1. Using the file “828 - Percent of Plastic Water Bottles.pdf”, determine the % of bottles that are made from PET, assuming the figure is for 2024. The % made from PET is expected to increase by 1 percent every year, compounded (i.e., each year’s value is 1.01× the prior year), until 2030 (e.g., 50% would grow to 50.5% in one year). Round answer to one decimal place when displayed in % (e.g., 50.51% would round to 50.5%).
   2. Using the calculation in 2a, determine the global annual demand for PET plastic bottles from 2024-2030 in metric tons, rounded to the nearest whole number. This will serve as a proxy for the total volume of PET potentially available for recycling.

🍶 Anchor Example 2
Flavored Water Market Entry Anchor Example
with Golden Response (Prompt, Sources, Golden Response, Rubric)


Prompt
FrancyBev is one of the top three vertically-integrated American soft-drink manufacturers.
FrancyBev is thinking of launching a new flavored water product. The company has requested advice on whether they should do it, and if so, how they should do it.


Review the market research provided by the company in source customersurvey.csv and identify an insight that FrancyBev should consider in their decision.


Next, FrancyBev wants to understand the potential opportunity and has requested an estimate of market size for sparkling bottled water. Use the sparkling water market by volume from 2011 to 2016 in source USAtoday.pdf as a proxy for growth through to 2026. State the CAGR and inputs and then estimate the number of 16-oz bottles of sparkling water that will be sold in the US in 2026, as an integer value.


FrancyBev estimates that the start-up launch costs (R&D, marketing) will be $4,750,000 and production costs are $0.90 per bottle. Assuming the retail price would be $1 per bottle, how many bottles would FrancyBev have to sell in order to break even? If FrancyBev was able to sell this amount in 2026, what would be FrancyBev's market share as a percentage, rounded to one decimal place?


To support production, FrancyBev is considering opening a new site for the new product. The company wants the new site to be as close as possible to their headquarters in Eastern Pennsylvania (near Philadelphia) and anticipates the facility would be 238,000 sq. ft. Assuming the costs scale proportionally, calculate the likely total annual operating costs based on the information in the Boyd report, as an integer value. How many bottles would FrancyBev have to sell in order to break even with the new site? If FrancyBev was able to sell this amount in 2026, what would be FrancyBev's market share as a percentage, rounded to one decimal place?


Sources
Bodyreport.pdf
Consumersurvey.csv
USAtoday.pdf


Golden Response
Key insights that FrancyBev should consider in their decision to enter the flavored water market:
1. There is a slight preference towards sparkling (57% of survey respondents' preference) vs. flat (43%) flavored water
2. There is room in the competitive landscape as 49% of the market is fragmented; 49% of survey respondents cited "another brand" as their preferred flavored water brand. 26% of respondents named Butters and 25% of respondents named Applelite.


Estimate the number of 16-oz bottles of sparkling water that will be sold in the US in 2026:
1. Identify the sparkling water market volume figures for 2011 and 2016
- 2011: 263m
- 2016: 574m


2. Calculate the CAGR of the sparkling water market by volume from 2011 to 2016:
(574,000,000 / 263,000,000) ^ (1/5) -1 = 16.9%


3. Calculate the sparkling water market volume in 2026:
574,000,000 ^ ((1 + 16.9%)^10)) = 2,735,627,772


4. Convert the 2026 volume from gallons to 16-oz bottles to find the number of 16-oz bottles of sparkling water that will be sold in the US in 2026:
2,735,627,772 * 128 oz/gal = 350,160,354,808
350,160,354,808 / 16 = 21,885,022,176


Number of bottles FrancyBev would have to sell in order to break even:
$4,750,000 / ($1 - $0.90) = 47,500,000


If FrancyBev were to sell this amount in 2026, FrancyBev's market share would be 47,500,000 / 21,885,022,176 = 0.2%


Calculate the likely total annual operating costs based on the information in the Boyd report:
1. Choose the benchmark as Camden NJ, as this is the closest site to Philadelphia
2. Calculate the scaling factor. 238,000 / 300,000 = 79%
3. Total annual operating cost is estimated as 79% * $43,082,181 = $34,178,530


Number of bottles FrancyBev would have to sell in order to break even with the new site:
($34,178,530 + $4,750,000) / ($1 - $0.90) = 389,285,303


This number of bottles represents 1.8% of 2026 market share (389,285,303 / 21,885,022,176)
